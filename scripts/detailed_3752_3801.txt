EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Batch 3752–3801 covers 50 essential English words spanning foundational grammar,
closed-class function words (pronouns, prepositions, modal verbs), days/months,
and polysemic high-frequency vocabulary.

The linguistic audit detected and resolved five massive clusters of systematic bugs:
1. Catastrophic Part-of-Speech & False Sense Collapses:
   - Word 3774 ("put"): The verb "put" was translated as a NOUN meaning "place / location"
     across 11 languages (Danish, Finnish, Swedish, Croatian, Latvian, Arabic, Vietnamese,
     Thai, Korean, Japanese, Kazakh)!
   - Word 3782 ("focus on"): The phrasal verb was translated as "juice / chemical concentrate"
     in 6 languages (Czech, Bulgarian, Hungarian, Slovenian, Estonian, Lithuanian), with
     Hungarian literally translating it as tomato paste/concentrate ("sűrítmény")!
   - Word 3769 ("promise to"): Translated as the singular abstract noun "a promise"
     (cs: slib, sk: sľub, fr: promesse, it: promessa, ro: promisiune, hu: ígéret, kk: уәде,
     el: υπόσχεση, etc.) in 20+ locales instead of the verbal infinitive pattern "promise to [do]".
   - Word 3768 ("during"): Translated as "for" or "because" across 9 European languages
     (ro: pentru, bg: за, cs: pro, sk: pre, hu: mert, el: για, sl: za, et: eest, lt: už)!
   - Word 3780 ("shall"): Czech had "mošt" (apple cider / must) due to a false friend
     confusion between "must" and German "Most"! Romanian had "necesitate" (noun), and Slovak
     had inflected 3rd person singular "musí".
2. Pronoun Case & Morphological Corruption:
   - Word 3798 ("me"): 14 languages had the nominative subjective pronoun "I" (it: io,
     tr: ben, ro: eu, bg: аз, cs: já, sk: ja, hu: én, el: εγώ, sl: jaz, et: mina, lt: aš,
     fi: minä, ar: أنا, kk: мен) instead of the objective pronoun "me / to me".
   - Word 3789 ("them"): 9 languages had the possessive determiner "their" (da: deres,
     fi: heidän, sv: deras, hr: njihov, vi: của họ, th: ของพวกเขา, ko: 그들의, ja: 彼らの, kk: олардың).
   - Word 3785 ("whose"): 8 languages only supplied relative pronoun forms and completely
     missed the primary interrogative pronoun "whose is this?" (cs: čí, pl: czyj, fr: à qui / de qui).
   - Croatian Dative/Locative Case Glitch: Croatian raw dump systematically contained oblique
     inflections instead of nominative headwords across 12 words (3755: učinku; 3756: drugome;
     3757: europskom; 3758: aspektu; 3761: vjeri, uvjerenju; 3762: prestrašenom; 3766: nekome;
     3770: rođenom; 3771: hrani; 3773: mogućem; 3776: mami; 3777: travnju). All restored to nominative.
3. Polysemy Failures:
   - Word 3791 ("fall"): Over 20 languages contained only "autumn" or only the verb "to drop",
     missing the dual identity of the word in standard English.
   - Word 3756 ("second"): Standardized to cover both ordinal (2nd) and temporal (1/60 min) meanings.
   - Word 3772 ("shoot"): Covers both discharging firearms and filming/photographing; German fixed
     from raw exclamation "Feuer" to "schießen, abfeuern; drehen, filmen".
   - Word 3792 ("March"): Covers both the third month and the marching action.
4. Homonym Traps & Calendar Collisions:
   - Word 3773 ("may"): Spanish had "mayo" and Polish had "maj" (month of May). Purged and calibrated
     strictly to modal verb senses (possibility / permission).
   - Word 3796 ("wow"): Greek raw dump had "Εκπληκτική επιτυχία" ("stunning success"!) instead of
     the interjection "ουάου, πω πω".
   - Word 3797 ("from"): Finnish had "keneltä, keneltä tahansa" ("from whom, from whomever"!) instead
     of the proper locative ablative/elative forms.
5. Capitalization & Register Standards:
   - Days and months (January 3764, Monday 3765, April 3777, March 3792): De-capitalized for
     Romance and Slavic languages where capitalization is ungrammatical (e.g. es: enero, lunes,
     abril, marzo; fr: janvier, lundi, avril, mars; it: gennaio, lunedì, aprile, marzo).
   - Mom (3776): Calibrated from formal "Mother" (Mutter, Mère, Madre) to warm, authentic informal
     maternal terms (Mama, Maman, Mamãe, Máma, Maminka, Anyu, Mamytė, etc.).
