EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Batch 4052–4101 contains high-frequency vocabulary, functional idioms, phrasal
verb patterns, irregular verbs (be, have, say, get, make, pay), numbers, and
nature/everyday nouns.

The linguistic audit identified and resolved seven massive systemic bug clusters:

1. The "Reset" Catastrophe for "shed" (ID 4092):
   - Across 13 European languages (French: "réinitialiser", Polish: "nastawić",
     Italian: "reset", Portuguese: "reiniciar", Romanian: "resetare", Bulgarian:
     "нулиране", Czech: "resetovat", Slovak: "resetovať", Hungarian: "visszaállítás",
     Greek: "επαναφορά", Slovenian: "ponastavi", Estonian: "lähtesta", Lithuanian:
     "iš naujo nustatyti"), the verb "shed" (cast off / drop leaves, skin, fur,
     tears) was mistranslated as "RESET" (reboot / restore system setting)!
   - In Urdu, it was merely transliterated as "شڈ".
   - Calibrated in all languages to authentic shedding / molting / casting off verbs
     (fr: perdre, muer; pl: zrzucać, linieć; it: perdere, mutare; pt: soltar, perder pelos;
     cs: shazovat, línat; ur: جھاڑنا، اتارنا، کینچلی بدلنا; kk: түлеу, тастау, төгу).

2. The "Beat a Retreat" & Profanity Explosion in "beat it" (ID 4066):
   - 14 European languages mistranslated the colloquial idiom "beat it" ("scram, get
     lost, go away") as "step back / withdraw" (German: Zurücktreten, French: reculez,
     Polish: cofnąć się, Italian: indietreggiare, Turkish: geri çekil, Portuguese: recue,
     Romanian: dați-vă înapoi, Bulgarian: отдръпни се, Czech: ustoupit, Slovak: ustúpiť,
     Hungarian: visszahúzódni, Greek: πίσω, Estonian: tagasi astuma, Lithuanian: atsitraukti).
   - Extreme vulgar profanity was detected and removed: Finnish had "painu vittuun",
     Swedish had "dra åt helvete", Croatian had "odjebi", Latvian had "aiziet d*cā".
     These were calibrated to authentic non-vulgar colloquial dismissals (fi: painu tiehesi,
     häivy; sv: stick, dra iväg; hr: gubi se, tornjaj se; lv: tinies, vācies prom).
   - Bengali had translated "beat" as musical rhythm / physical strike ("তাল, আঘাত করা").
     Calibrated to "এখান থেকে কেটে পড়ো, দূর হও".
   - Kazakh had literally translated it as "nickname" ("лақап ат"). Calibrated to
     "жоғал, тайып тұр, кет әрмен".

3. "Inquire" vs "Request" Confusion in "ask for" (ID 4093):
   - Nearly 20 languages mistranslated "ask for" as "ask a question / inquire / interrogate"
     (fr: demander, s'enquérir; pl: pytać, dowiadywać się; it: chiedere, informarsi;
     pt: perguntar, indagar; ro: întreba; bg: питам, разпитвам; cs: zeptat se;
     sk: opýtať sa, informovať sa; hu: kérdez, érdeklődik; el: ρωτώ; sl: vprašati;
     et: küsima, pärima; lt: klausti, teirautis; da: spørge, forhøre sig; fi: kysyä, tiedustella;
     sv: fråga, förhöra; hr: pitati, raspitati se; lv: jautāt, interesēties; hi: पूछना, पूछताछ करना;
     ar: يسأل، يستفسر; vi: hỏi, tìm hiểu; th: ถาม สอบถาม; ko: 묻다, 문의하다; ja: 尋ねる、問い合わせる;
     bn: জিজ্ঞাসা করা).
   - All restored to true request semantics: request / demand / solicit something
     (e.g., pedir, solicitar; bitten um; prosić o; a cere; bede om; pyytää; be om;
     मांगना; يطلب; تقاضا کردن; 요청하다; 求める; 请求、索要; сұрау, өтіну).

4. Literal "Inanimate / Lifeless" Glitch in "its" (ID 4077):
   - The grammatical prompt/gloss "неодушевленное" (inanimate) in Russian was literally
     translated into the target vocabulary as the word for "corpse / dead / inanimate"!
     Danish had "hans, hendes, livløse" (livløse = corpses / lifeless ones); Finnish had
     "hänen, eloton" (eloton = dead); Swedish had "hans, hennes, livlösa"; Czech had
     "jeho, její, neživý"; Slovak had "neživý"; Bulgarian had "неодушевен"; Arabic had
     leaked prompt text "الخاص به، بها لغير العاقل بحسب السياق".
   - All calibrated to clean possessive determiners (da: dens, dets; fi: sen; sv: dess;
     cs: jeho, její; es: su, sus; ar: الخاص به، الخاص بها).

5. "What is the difference?" Fallacy in "who cares" (ID 4068):
   - Because Russian colloquially uses "какая разница" for "who cares", automated translation

     Finnish (mikä on ero), Swedish (vad är skillnaden), Croatian (koja je razlika),

   - Calibrated to authentic idioms meaning "nobody cares / who cares / so what"
     (da: hvem bekymrer sig; fi: ketä kiinnostaa, mitä väliä; sv: vem bryr sig;
     hr: koga briga; lv: kam tas rūp; hi: किसे परवाह है, क्या फ़र्क पड़ता है;
     id: siapa peduli, masa bodoh; vi: ai mà quan tâm; th: ใครจะสน, ช่างมันเถอะ;
     ko: 알 게 뭐야, 누가 신경 써; ja: 誰が気にするもんか、知るか; kk: кімге бәрібір).
